## How to use this document

Five segments. Five landing pages. One product. Every paid social dollar maps to exactly one segment, one LP, and one message hierarchy. Creative can flex; the segment → LP pairing does not. All traffic converts to the same action: **join the waitlist** (first run sold out — scarcity is real, so we say it plainly, never manufacture it).

**Product facts every segment shares (memorize):** 1oz/30ml ready-to-pour liquid sachet · 2.5g ceremonial matcha · 2g grass-fed collagen peptides · 200mg organic lion's mane fruiting-body extract at ≥70% beta-glucans · ~60mg caffeine (+ naturally occurring L-theanine) · no powder, no whisk, no blender · flavors: vanilla & strawberry · ritual: pour / swirl / glow.

**Compliance baseline for ALL segments:** structure/function claims only, FDA disclaimer wherever claims appear, no disease/drug claims, no "clinically proven," no competitor names. Full rules: `Product/Compliance & Claims/claims-guidelines.md`.

## Segment 1 — the ritual girlies (aesthetic wellness)

**LP slug:** `/lp/ritual`

### Who they are
- **Demographics:** Women 18–28, urban/suburban, students + early-career creatives, $30–75k HHI, heavy TikTok/IG/Pinterest use, already buy $7 café matchas 3–5x/week
- **Psychographics:** Aesthetic-first. Their morning drink is content — the glass, the light, the swirl. Follow matcha-tok, "that girl" routines, romanticize-your-life culture. Brand loyalty flows from how a product looks and feels in their hands, then from taste, then from function. Quiet-luxury aspirational (Aesop on the shelf, Celine on the moodboard, Poppi in the fridge).

### Platforms
TikTok (primary — Spark Ads on matcha-tok style creative), Instagram Reels + Stories, Pinterest (idea pins: "matcha at home"). Meta Advantage+ lookalikes seeded off waitlist.

### Pains
- Home matcha is a whole production: whisk, sift, clump, disappoint
- Café matcha habit costs $150+/month and requires leaving the house
- Most at-home matcha tastes bitter/fishy because it's culinary grade
- Powdered "aesthetic" brands still photograph like homework

### Dream outcome
A morning that looks like the videos she saves — golden light, a slow green swirl into oat milk, a drink that tastes like the $8 café order — achieved in 15 seconds, every day, without skill.

### Objections → answers
| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Concentrate can't taste like real ceremonial matcha" | 2.5g first-harvest, shade-grown ceremonial — more real matcha than most cafés use |
| "Is this just another aesthetic brand with a pretty pouch" | full doses printed on the sachet; nothing hidden |
| "It's sold out, why should I care" | first run sold out = social proof; waitlist gets first access to the next pour |

### Key message hierarchy
1. **The swirl** — the most beautiful moment in matcha, now the easiest (hero visual, always)
2. Ceremonial without the ceremony — first-harvest, shade-grown, zero whisk
3. Actually tastes like the café order (smooth, never bitter)
4. It's also functional (collagen + lion's mane) — mention, don't lead
5. Sold out; join the waitlist for the next pour

### Hook angles (test all, kill fast)
1. "the matcha girlies were right about everything except the whisk" (text-on-swirl ASMR)
2. POV: your 7am looks like her 7am now (pour into glass of oat milk, golden light, zero VO)
3. "I stopped spending $8 on matcha and my morning got *prettier*" (receipt math + swirl)
4. ASMR: tear → pour → swirl → sip. no words. let the plume do the talking
5. "ceremonial matcha has a dress code. we removed it." (deadpan, whisk in trash — playful)
6. "things in my kitchen that just make sense: the sachet edition" (trend format)
7. carousel: 5 stills of the swirl in sequence, caption "pour / swirl / glow"

### Compliant claim language
- **DO:** "ceremonial-grade, first-harvest matcha" · "smooth, never bitter" · "provides antioxidant support" · "supports healthy energy levels" · taste/aesthetic/convenience claims are unregulated — lean on them hard here
- **DON'T:** "detoxifies" · "the healthiest way to start your day" (implied superiority/health outcome) · "better than [café/brand]" · any implication matcha treats anxiety or any condition
- Include FDA disclaimer on LP + in ad caption only if a structure/function claim appears in that specific ad. Pure taste/ritual ads need no disclaimer — keep most ads in this segment claim-free by design.

## Segment 2 — the glow getters (collagen-glow beauty seekers)

**LP slug:** `/lp/glow`

### Who they are
- **Demographics:** Women 22–38, skew 25–34, $50–120k HHI, spend $80+/month on skincare, buy Bloom/Vital Proteins-type ingestibles, sephora-loyal, IG + TikTok "skinfluencer" audiences
- **Psychographics:** Treat beauty as a system: SPF, retinal, supplements, sleep. Believe "beauty is a health outcome." Skeptical of miracle products but loyal to routines with visible logic. Read ingredient lists. Hate that collagen powder is a chore (clumpy scoop, extra step).

### Platforms
Instagram (primary — Reels + Stories, beauty creator whitelisting), TikTok (skincare-tok, "glow routine" formats), Pinterest (beauty routine boards). Meta interest stacks: collagen, ingestible beauty, Sephora, skin barrier.

### Pains
- Collagen powder is one more scoop, one more shaker, one more thing to remember
- Ingestible beauty tastes like punishment
- Supplement fatigue: cabinets of half-finished tubs
- Doesn't trust brands that hide doses in "beauty blends"

### Dream outcome
Her morning drink quietly becomes a beauty step — grass-fed collagen + antioxidant-rich matcha in something she'd order at a café anyway. Consistency finally feels effortless, and effortless is the luxury.

### Objections → answers
| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "2g of collagen isn't enough" | 2g of low-molecular-weight hydrolyzed peptides (Type I & III) as a *daily maintenance dose* — the dose you'll actually take every day beats the scoop you skip |
| "Collagen drinks taste chalky" | it's a matcha latte first; the collagen is hydrolyzed and dissolved — you taste vanilla or strawberry, not supplement |
| "Is it clean?" | grass-fed, pasture-raised bovine; no added sugar, no artificial sweeteners, no proprietary blends |

### Key message hierarchy
1. Your matcha latte is now a beauty step — 2g grass-fed collagen in every pour
2. Beauty is a health outcome: collagen supports skin health; matcha provides antioxidant support
3. Doses on the label (Type I & III, hydrolyzed, low molecular weight — science that doesn't sound like science)
4. Tastes like a treat, works like a routine
5. First run sold out — waitlist for the next pour

### Hook angles
1. "my skincare routine has 9 steps. this one you drink." (vanity flat-lay → pour)
2. "collagen powder girlies… we need to talk about the scoop" (scoop in trash, sachet tear)
3. "the lazy girl's ingestible beauty routine" (15-second pour, done)
4. ingredient receipt format: "2g grass-fed collagen. 2.5g ceremonial matcha. 0g of stuff you can't pronounce."
5. "I put my collagen IN my matcha so I never skip either" (routine-stacking logic)
6. AM routine ASMR: cleanser → serum → SPF → *pour / swirl / glow*
7. "beauty from a sachet sounds fake until you read the label" (label macro shot)

### Compliant claim language
- **DO:** "supports skin health" · "supports gut health" · "2g grass-fed hydrolyzed collagen peptides" · "provides antioxidant support" · "Type I & III collagen" (factual) · "bioavailable peptides" (factual, substantiated)
- **DON'T:** "anti-aging" · "erases fine lines / wrinkles" · "clinically proven glow" · "younger skin in 30 days" · before/after skin photos implying treatment outcomes · "heals your gut"
- This segment WILL make structure/function claims in most ads → FDA disclaimer in every caption + on LP. Never let creators promise visible skin results on a timeline.

## Segment 3 — the coffee breakup (anxious-coffee quitters wanting calm energy)

**LP slug:** `/lp/calm`

### Who they are
- **Demographics:** All genders 24–40, skew female 60/40, office/hybrid knowledge workers, $50–110k HHI, 2+ coffees/day, active on TikTok + Reddit wellness spaces, Oura/WHOOP-curious
- **Psychographics:** Love coffee, hate what it does to them — the 2pm crash, the racing heart, the doomscroll jitters. Have tried quitting and relapsed because decaf is sad and cold-turkey hurts. Want an off-ramp, not a lecture. Respond to honest "I switched" narratives over hype.

### Platforms
TikTok (primary — "I quit coffee" narrative formats), Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts (30-day switch content), Reddit-adjacent Meta interest targeting (decaf, caffeine sensitivity, mushroom coffee).

### Pains
- Afternoon crash and morning-anxiety spiral from coffee
- Caffeine dependence + terrible sleep, but zero-caffeine feels impossible
- Mushroom "coffee alternatives" taste like dirt and hide their doses
- Wants energy that feels smooth, not spiky

### Dream outcome
Steady, calm alertness from wake-up through the afternoon — no crash, no jitters, no 3pm regret — from a drink they genuinely look forward to instead of a sacrifice they endure.

### Objections → answers
| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "60mg won't be enough caffeine for me" | ~60mg (about half an espresso shot) plus matcha's naturally occurring L-theanine — smoother absorption curve, sustained not spiked; many switchers taper with one coffee + one shroomé |
| "Matcha tastes like lawn" | ceremonial grade is the difference — smooth and naturally sweet, in vanilla or strawberry |
| "Mushroom drinks are a scam" | doses printed on every sachet: 200mg lion's mane fruiting body, ≥70% beta-glucans, third-party tested |

### Key message hierarchy
1. Break up with the crash — not with caffeine (60mg + L-theanine = calm, steady energy)
2. The caffeine curve story: coffee spikes, matcha sustains (visualize it)
3. Supports sustained focus and healthy energy levels — structure/function framing, always
4. Tastes like an upgrade, not a compromise
5. Sold out first run — join the waitlist before the next pour

### Hook angles
1. "day 14 without coffee and I owe it all to a sachet" (journal format, honest tone)
2. split screen: 2pm on coffee vs 2pm on shroomé (comedic crash vs calm typing)
3. "I didn't quit caffeine. I quit the crash." (text-on-pour)
4. the caffeine curve drawn on a napkin next to the latte (education-style hook)
5. "POV: it's 3pm and you feel… fine?" (quiet, unsettlingly calm office shot)
6. "what 60mg of caffeine + L-theanine actually feels like" (green-screen explainer)
7. "my heart isn't racing and I don't know what to do with all this peace" (self-aware humor)

### Compliant claim language
- **DO:** "supports healthy energy levels" · "supports sustained focus" · "no crash" as a caffeine-curve/experience statement tied to caffeine + L-theanine · "~60mg caffeine" (factual) · personal-experience creator content with "individual results may vary"
- **DON'T:** "cures caffeine addiction/withdrawal" · "treats anxiety" or "calms anxiety" (disease/mental-health claim — use "calm, steady energy," never anxiety language in claims) · "natural alternative to [medication]" · "fixes your sleep" · "healthier than coffee" as a health-outcome claim
- Highest-risk segment for accidental disease claims (anxiety, dependence, sleep). Brief every creator explicitly: describe the *feeling*, never a condition. FDA disclaimer on LP + in any claim-bearing caption.

## Segment 4 — the deep workers (focus / lion's-mane brain performance)

**LP slug:** `/lp/focus`

### Who they are
- **Demographics:** All genders 22–40, skew male 55/45 (our only male-leaning segment), students, engineers, founders, creators, traders, gamers; $40–150k HHI; YouTube + X + TikTok "productivity" audiences
- **Psychographics:** Optimizers. Read labels like spec sheets, know the mycelium-vs-fruiting-body debate, follow Huberman-style content, own a nootropic graveyard drawer. They buy *verified numbers*, not vibes — but they still appreciate that this doesn't look like a gamer supplement.

### Platforms
YouTube Shorts + pre-roll (primary for trust-building), TikTok (supplement-tok, "read the label" formats), X (founder-voice + spec-sheet memes), Meta interest stacks: nootropics, lion's mane, deep work, Notion/productivity.

### Pains
- Most lion's mane products are mycelium-on-grain — mostly starch, doses hidden
- Nootropic stacks are complicated, pill-heavy, and taste like a chemistry set
- Coffee gives focus with a side of jitters and a hard crash mid-deep-work
- "Proprietary blend" is a red flag they're tired of seeing

### Dream outcome
A single, honest, spec-sheet-clean drink that anchors a locked-in morning work block: lion's mane at a disclosed dose and verified beta-glucan content, plus matcha's caffeine + L-theanine — with zero prep friction breaking flow.

### Objections → answers
| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "200mg lion's mane is a small dose" | 200mg of ≥70% beta-glucan fruiting-body extract ≈ more actives than gram-level doses of 15–30% mycelium products — concentration beats mass; third-party verified |
| "Is this fruiting body or mycelium?" | fruiting body, hot-water extracted, ≥70% beta-glucans, printed on the label |
| "Why is there collagen in my nootropic" | 2g protein-adjacent peptides, neutral in taste; skip the debate — everything is disclosed, nothing is filler |

### Key message hierarchy
1. Read the label: 200mg lion's mane fruiting body, ≥70% beta-glucans, third-party tested (lead with the spec)
2. Caffeine + L-theanine (~60mg + naturally occurring theanine): supports sustained focus
3. No proprietary blends, no fairy-dusting — every dose disclosed
4. 15-second prep = zero friction before deep work
5. First run sold out — waitlist is the queue

### Hook angles
1. "your lion's mane gummy is mostly rice. here's the number they don't print." (label exposé)
2. "the 3 numbers to check before buying any mushroom product" (checklist format — mirrors TikTok Script 5)
3. "I put my morning stack on one label" (pill bottles swept aside, one sachet remains)
4. "≥70% beta-glucans. most brands won't even tell you theirs." (spec-sheet aesthetic)
5. "my 4-hour deep work block starts with a 15-second pour" (desk POV timelapse)
6. "fruiting body vs mycelium, explained in one pour" (green-screen + b-roll)
7. "we third-party test so you don't have to trust us" (receipts energy, lab report b-roll)

### Compliant claim language
- **DO:** "supports sustained focus" · "supports immune function" (beta-glucans) · "200mg lion's mane fruiting-body extract" · "≥70% beta-glucan content, third-party tested" · "higher beta-glucan concentration than typical mushroom supplements" (approved comparative)
- **DON'T:** "boosts brainpower / memory / neurogenesis" · "NGF" mechanism claims · "prevents cognitive decline" (disease claim) · "better than Adderall" or any drug comparison (hard prohibition) · "clinically proven nootropic" · naming competitor brands in label comparisons
- This audience rewards precision — compliant language IS the flex here ("supports sustained focus. that's the claim. here's the dose."). FDA disclaimer on LP + claim-bearing captions.

## Cross-segment operating rules

1. **One segment per ad set.** Never blend messages; the algorithm and the copy both get muddy.
2. **Honest scarcity only.** "first run sold out. next pour drops soon." is true — say it exactly like that. No fake countdown timers, no "only 12 left" theater. (Email keeps its no-urgency rule per `../Email/email-strategy.md`; paid LPs may state real sold-out status because it is a fact, not a tactic.)
3. **Disclaimer discipline.** Any ad or LP with a structure/function claim carries: *"These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."*
4. **Creator content** = FTC #ad disclosure + "individual results may vary" on experience narratives + no disease language, contractually.
5. **Measurement:** each LP fires the same waitlist event with a `segment` UTM (`utm_campaign=lp-ritual` etc.) so Klaviyo can segment welcome flows by entry story.
6. **Naming:** brand renders as "shroomé" in display copy, lowercase always; URLs and UTMs use plain "shroome."
